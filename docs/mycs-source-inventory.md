# Whole-source MyCS collection and classroom adaptation

Reviewed October 14, 2026. The scope is the whole public SFUSD MyCS teaching
collection, not only Robot Dress-Up. The result is a reviewed curriculum
adaptation inside Julio's existing courses, not an indiscriminate website or
third-party asset mirror.

## Coverage

- **103 teacher pages:** all 80 instructional and assessment pages are covered.
  The other 23 are reviewed overviews, navigation dividers and the Resources
  directory, not omitted lessons.
- **103 student-site URLs:** 82 returned public curriculum content and 21
  returned stale/unavailable responses. Current navigation and linked handouts
  recover several outdated teacher links without authentication.
- **130 public curriculum documents:** blank handouts, planners, rubrics and
  curriculum references were retrieved and reviewed. Answer keys, worked
  Assessment Guides, forms, student submissions and media are not published.
- **80 additional activities:** Units 0 through 6, Maze, capstones, computing,
  binary, internet and optional physical computing are distributed across
  existing Scratch Level 1 and Scratch Level 2 modules. Relevant student-only
  variants are included. The earlier credited Dress-Up guide remains in place.
- **85 linked Scratch project IDs:** exact source pages and labels, including
  CodeStart versus ModelOnly roles, are inventoried separately from copied
  source files. Their original code/assets have not been imported.

The machine-readable [source inventory](mycs-source-inventory.json) records
every reviewed page, content hash, coverage decision, document observation,
project reference and remaining input. A linked project is not represented as
a verified download, licensed archive or current working example merely
because its URL was found.

## Commitment and previous delivery

In the September 26 class, the discussion called for preserving the teacher's
resources and breaking them into a gradual progression: a working example,
guided additions, then independent practice. September 30 reinforced that
model; October 7 demonstrated the local starters and their classroom download
workflow. The complete resource collection was not delivered by those original
starters alone.

The earlier implementation already supplied twelve original Scratch starters,
including Robot Dress-Up, with Normal, Hard, and concrete checks. Their files,
artwork, identifiers, and existing licenses are unchanged by this update.

## Integration into existing courses

The original course IDs, six Scratch Level 1 modules, seventeen Scratch Level
2 modules and twelve local core starters are preserved. New activities are
explicit choices in their appropriate existing modules' Supplemental view,
not new classes, course entries or archived-reference substitutes. All have
Model, Build, Hard and Check directions. They use an existing original starter
or a blank local workspace when appropriate; unplugged and unsupported
hardware activities do not falsely launch an IDE project.

The progression includes original assets and guided rebuilding rather than
silently claiming that external starter files have been copied. Examples now
cover events and sprite dialogue; animation/art/sound; races, pointer sensing,
Boolean launch controls and line-following; four-stage maze development;
story variables, game choices, multiplication, Flappy-style motion, lists and
quizzes; reusable animation/shape procedures and generative art; research,
midterm and final-project planning; and computing/binary/internet foundations.

Actual handouts informed details such as colored line-following sensors,
pilot/safety launch conditions, five maze treasures and next-level messages,
shopping-cart Add/Remove mode, and Jump(height)/Spin(degrees). Obsolete Scratch
2 navigation, source link/title mismatches, polygon-angle mistakes, stale
statistics and copied closeout text are not repeated as authoritative facts.
School surveys, required accounts/studios, cloud scores, personal prompts and
YouTube-to-MP3 instructions are not restored.
